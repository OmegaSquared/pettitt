The Clock · Asset Management

Navigating the business cycle.

Buy-and-hold is a static model subject to the whims of the market. We manage capital through the lens of the business cycle and strive to shift your positioning before the market forces your hand. Wealth isn’t just about capturing the upside — it’s about surviving the downside.

01

Recovery

The economy stabilizes after a recession. Earnings and consumer confidence begin a slow climb.

02

Early Expansion

Growth accelerates as credit expands and production increases. A strong environment for risk assets.

03

Mid Expansion

The economy reaches full stride. Profitability is high and credit is widely available.

04

Late Expansion

Growth slows and inflation begins to rise. Risks shift as the cycle nears its historical peak.

05

Approaching Recession

Indicators peak and begin to roll over. Transitioning to defensive positioning becomes critical.

06

Recession

A period of contraction and peak uncertainty. Focus shifts to capital preservation and liquidity.

The Asymmetry of Risk

Recovering from large losses is harder than most investors think.

Conventional wisdom says “stay the course.” Math says otherwise. Avoiding catastrophic losses is one of the most important factors in long-term compounding.

A 50% loss requires a 100% gain just to break even. Catastrophic market losses are not random events — historically they are concentrated within economic recessions. That is why identifying the stage of the business cycle is our first priority: avoiding a recessionary drawdown can be the difference between consistent compounding and spending years merely trying to get back to even.

Foundational Logic

The intertwined feedback loop.

Economic stability is driven by a self-reinforcing loop of income, consumption, production and employment. Our framework watches for the points where momentum shifts from expansion to inflection.

Positive Feedback Loop

  • Rising income
  • More consumption
  • More production
  • More employment
  • Rising income … and the cycle reinforces itself

Negative Feedback Loop

  • Falling income
  • Lower consumption
  • Lower production
  • Lower employment
  • Falling income … and contraction feeds on itself
